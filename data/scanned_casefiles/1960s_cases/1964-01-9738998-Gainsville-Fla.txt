Dear Sirs:

On Sunday, January 26, 1964, I observed a yellowish oblong light (called SSS in reference material) pass over my vicinity and then pass out of sight approximately at the same altitude as a small private plane. I observed a small moving path, and at dusk, with the fuel sunset, were the only object visible in the blue-orange sky.

My son and I discussed, since there was only one sighted light visible, and that it appeared to glow as a jet aircraft or the like, that it must be above all air space vehicle, i.e., weather satellite, rocket, or some type of space-type body.

We would appreciate your assistance in identifying strictly the nature of the object, its purpose, and its or initial plan if one exists. The observation I made was from (approximately) Latitude 29°N, Longitude 77°W at 70 miles west to S. Gainsville, Florida.

We are in assurance for your assistance.

February 29, 1964

Dear Mr. Weiss:

This is in response to your letter of January 25, 1954 in which you reported observing an unidentified flying object.

Since this object had a southwesterly component, it could not have been a satellite. Although there are no high altitude air routes over Gainsville the duration of your sighting (ten minutes) is not excessive for an aircraft at high altitude, particularly since you indicate that it crossed the zenith. If the object was an aircraft, it was not on a regular schedule. However, this does not rule out aircraft as the most likely cause of your sighting.

Enclosed is a brochure concerning the Air Force program on Unidentified Flying Objects which may be of interest to you.

Thank you for reporting this observation to the Air Force.

Sincerely,
