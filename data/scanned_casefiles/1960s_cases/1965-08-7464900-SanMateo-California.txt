Department of the Air Force

Dear Sirs:

In the course of a conversation the other day, a person mentioned the sighting of an unidentified flying object to me. Since I do have a rather large interest in this field, I interviewed the person who was involved in the sighting. I thought you might be interested in his answers to several questions which I put to him.

Question #1 was-What were the conditions under which you saw the object in the sky? The answer was that it was a clear night with a few clouds."It was about 9:30 P.M. two weeks ago when I first spotted the object in the sky. It seemed to be shining as from the reflected light of the sun. It had a fantastic speed. As it reached the zenith, four pink objects shot out of it, one to each point of the compass. The pink objects were going at a fantastic rate of speed. As the white object continued to descend, it stopped 30° above the horizon."

Question #2 was-Did anything occur when the object stopped? Answer was-"The object changed color from reddish-pink, to green, to yellowish-orange.

Question #3 was-Next what happened? Answer was that the object kept changing colors and then suddenly disappeared.

Question #4 was- Was their any noise or sound that caught your attention? Answer was negative.

EXAMINATION OF PHOTO B:

Camera position: By observation, it was obvious that the photo was not of object D as first reported. There is no pole in the proper position from anywhere near the observation scene. From the NW corner of 78th and Main the pole would be in the correct position, but the lighting and wires different. Thus, the only possible alternative is that the photo was of object A in the West. The approximate camera position was determined by memory and observation of the apparent positions of the cross wires with respect to the pole. Thus it was determined that the camera position was approximately 134 feet east and between 52' 55"N and 51'N south of the pole, elevated on a ladder 5'7" high, with the camera pointing at about a 25° angle. The three test photos (photo 1) were taken from 21'4"N, 22'52"E and 32'8" south of the pole, determining that the correct position is approx. 52° S south.

GENERAL COMMENTS: It is interesting that all witnesses
were quite convinced that the photo B had been
taken facing north, a complete possibility.
Even the photographer's records, written immediately
after the sighting, give no indication that a
photo was taken facing east that includes a star
and pole, although one is indicated for the north.
This sighting and investigation points up the need
for on the spot investigations as soon as possible
after the sighting to minimize the need of relying
on the memory of a witness. Even though the incident
is still vivid in my mind, it is most frustrating that
I can remember the star-pole photo taken facing north
(which was never returned by the newspaper) as if
I took it yesterday, but can not, even in the face of
this investigation, recall a similar photo taken facing
west. Perhaps had the investigatin been conducted
sooner after the sighting this photo puzzle could
have been solved, or perhaps it would never have
existed.

Meteorologist Claims
UFOs Just Reflections

DALLAS, Tex.(AP)—Dale Milford, radio and television meteorologist, said Wednesday he believes he has found the source of at least a large number of the unidentified flying objects—UFO's—.

reported in recent days. Milford, meteorologist for station KRLD, said he found confirmation of his theory to-day after an airplane flight in search of the phenomenon. His theory is that the UFO's are the reflection of light from a warmer layer of air between two cooler layers, called an inversion. The inversions can be seen by weather bureau radar.

Jo and Kent Bridie of the Dallas Morning News flew at 9,500 feet early Wednesday morning over a Dallas suburb, Richardson, to which they were guided by ground reports of flying objects and by the U.S. Weather Bureau.

Milford reported that he saw what could be called a "flying object." He said it was the reflection of a star.

The meteorologist said the object moved back and forth and changed direction horizontally. Milford said at one period the object, by reflection, remained stationary for two minutes.
