1. Description of Object: The observers, Central Fire Control gunner and right waist gunner on a B-50 type aircraft, noted a silver object in the wake of a C-82 type aircraft while they were under mock attack by a B-29 aircraft during a routine training mission. Positive observations indicated the object was observed in the slipstream behind the aircraft as a silver ball with possible flat surfaces evidenced by variations in reflections and characteristics. When the other observer bellowed it is on a plane like a disc, which first showed its circular side then its flat side. The object's apparent diameter was three (3) feet and its distance from the observers was estimated at twenty (20) miles. The ball appeared to remain stationary during the entire sighting. The object made no audible sound.

2. Time: Sighting began at 1100 hours, PST, 2 February 1953, and the object remained in view for approximately 5-7 (3) minutes thereafter.

3. Manner of Observation: The observation was made visually from the Central Fire Control gunner's position on top, the fuselage and from the right gunner's blister on the right side of the fuselage of a B-50 type aircraft. The only items utilized were unaided eyes. Attempt to obtain material evidence. Initial aberration was drawn by a Lt at the object was apparently silent. The two gunners were the only crew members to view the object from the aircraft.

4. Location: The observers were airborne at the time of sighting. The aircraft was cruising at 267 degrees and was in the vicinity of Victorville, California (approx. 34°34N 117 57W). The object was observed at 560 degrees, 40,000 to 43,000 feet in altitude, and approximately twenty (20) miles distant.

5. Observers:

   a. Technical Sergeant CARL Y. EDWARDS, USAF, the Central Fire Control gunner, has had fifty (50) hours flying time and has been in the Air Force for some (7) years. He is presently attached to the 330th Bombardment Squadron, 97d Bombardment Wing (M), Castle Air Force Base, California. He has not previously observed unidentified flying objects.

   b. Staff Sergeant KENNETH S. JOSLIN, USAF, the right gunner on the same crew as Edwards, has been on flying status since April, 1950 and has flown sixty (60) hours of flying time. His total time in the service has been five and one half (5½) years. Sergeant Joslin, also, has no previous sightings of unidentified flying objects.

At 1900, OCT. 2 February 1953, I was in the CPO gunner's position of a B-50 type aircraft, Number 9365, flying at an altitude of 25,000 feet. I was searching for fighter attacks from F-86 type aircraft which were in the area. I saw a B-52 type aircraft at three (3) o'clock high. As this aircraft passed, I noticed a silver object in its wake. I called on the interphone, "Does anyone see that silver object behind that B-52?" I was answered by my right gunner saying, "Yes". Neither one of us could identify the object. We both took gun camera pictures to confirm the sighting.

The object looked alike a silver disk, first showing its circular side and then the flat side. It first appeared high at three (3) o'clock and was left high at four-thirty (4:30). I watched until it went out of sight.

At 1900, GCT, 2 February 1953, I was occupying the right
gunner's position of B-50 type aircraft No. 9365 on a routine
training mission. During this period we were under a simulated
attack from four (4) F-86 type fighter aircraft from George AFB,
Victorville, California. I was searching for fighters in my
assigned quadran when a large jet aircraft leaving four contrails
appeared at three (3) o'clock high crossing over course to eight
(8) o'clock. As I observed this aircraft, I noticed a silver ball
off the right wing. The ball was stationary and we left it behind
at six (6) o'clock. Total time for this observation was about
three (3) minutes. The ball appeared to be made of polished metal,
silver or chrome. It appeared to be small, and due to this fact
I could not notice any flat surfaces.

During the sighting I took two sequences of pictures with my
G.S.A.F. camera. On the first sequence I turned the reticle out
so that only the object remained. In the second sequence I turned
the reticle on and placed the center dot on the left side of the
object.
