SUBJECT: Report on Indicated airborne target over Oak Ridge Area.

TO : Knoxville CAF Detachment
P. O. Box 431
Knoxville, Tenn

On 21 January 1951 at 1520 o'clock while on a practice flight of two F-82 aircraft the radar operator of aircraft 45-472, [redacted], observed the indication of an airborne target in the area of the guards. He notified the pilot, [redacted], of same who notified CAF. CAF gave the go ahead to investigate area. The pilot in flight lead, and on radio contact with his wingman, the information on the "0" scan (elevation) indicated the target was varying altitude between ten and twenty-five degrees above the horizon—sighting altitude which was 7,500 feet. At a range of approximately yards the indication at elevation began to descend until the limit of depression of the antenna was reached. At this point the wingman followed into a one o'clock altitude and the target was sighting up and initiated the CAD area plant of Oak Ridge. The intercepting aircraft then broke off the intercept and returned to Knoxville Air Base in a "Hurry" for the other aircraft. A short run was made in the area the "target" was again indicated and a second run made on same. At the completion of the flying period the radar observer reported again run over the area to again pick up the same type indication. The pilot started the run from approximately 30 miles and again at 17.5 miles the indication was received and locked on to. This time the run was completed to the limit of the antenna depression and then broken off because the line of flight again was cornered the Oak Ridge Plant. This phenomenon was duly reported to CAF and GSI.

SUMMARY OF INFORMATION

(Refer S/I Subject as above dated 20 January 1951 pertaining to the sighting at 2345 hours on 15 January 1951)

At approximately 1945 hours on 15 January 1951, an unusually brilliant object was sighted by five persons eating in the "Control Zone" of the Atomic Energy Project, a utility plant, Tennessee. Attached hereto is the statement concerning this phenomenon. The weather at the time of this sighting is substantially the same as reported in the previous report for 15 January 1951, as observed to above.

Because of the 653rd A C & W Squadron, (Radar), McHess (sic) town airport, Knoxville, Tennessee overall report that no aircraft were in the vicinity at the time of the observation, and the radar site was receiving no interference.

At 1220 hours on 21 January 1951, a radar sighting of an unidentified object over Oak Ridge, Tennessee was made by the airborne radar equipment installed in an F-82 fighter aircraft of the 319 th Fighter Squadron, 52nd Air Force Group, Lockes Tyson, airport, Knoxville, Tennessee. The radar interference of this target from various angles and found that the source of the interference originated from the area of the X-10 Plant. No visual reference to anything in the sky was reported by either the pilot or the radar operator of the F-82 aircraft.

The weather at the time of this incident is reportedly: "Partly cloudy, with fast winds at seven (7) miles per hour. Temperature 53 degrees, and dewpoint 19 degrees. No billows or other weather apparatus was in the sky at the time. Between the hours 1945 and 1315, the wind was variable from south to northeast at from calm to eleven (11) miles per hour.

SUBJECT: Unconventional Aircraft, ATI Project No. 10073

1. It is considered highly probable that the radar target detected three times by the airborne radar set in the F-82 aircraft on 21 January was a ground target in the vicinity of the X-10 plant.

2. The peculiar phenomena of having the target first appear at an altitude above the interceptor and then below the aircraft is considered to be due to the abnormal refraction of the radar electromagnetic emissions. The airborne radar transmitted energy was refracted to the ground by a "temperature and/or moisture duct" that was located at about the same altitude as the interceptor; more details are given in paragraph 3 below. When the interceptor closed on the target, the target first appeared to be above the interceptor until the apex of the tent radar "rays" was reached, end after that the target appeared below the interceptor.
