INTELLIGENCE (PRIORITY) REPORT
(Unidentified Flying Objects Reporting)
AFI 200-5

1. On 4 August 1952 at 1645 local time, Mr. [REDACTED], of [REDACTED] Village, Honolulu, T.H., observed two (2) objects in the sky which is believed to be flying discs.

2. Upon interrogation by the Intelligence Officer of the 1500th Air Base Wing, Mr. [REDACTED] stated that he observed two green colored disc-shaped objects about 6 to 8 inches in diameter hovering over Hickam Air Force Base in very close formation. The objects were shaped like a dinner plate with the center portion and tapering outward to a thin edge. These objects were observed in a spinning, rotating fashion at a rapid estimated speed of about 400 miles per hour. The objects were observed for a period of about 5 seconds [REDACTED] stated that one of the objects suddenly cleared immediately to the other object causing the other to disappear in a spinning motion and disappeared causing a disturbance of the air so that produced a wave-like motion as it sped away. There was no audible sound or noises emanating from the object. The outstanding recognition feature of the object was its plate-like appearance. It is estimated that the object was about the size of a conventional fighter plane.

3. The objects when reported were at an altitude of about 2,000 feet. Further interrogation revealed that the observer saw the objects at a 35° to 40° angle. No attempt was made at sighting. The distance from the observer to the objects could not be estimated at the time of the sighting. The observer stated that there were at least two planes in the vicinity of the objects. No report of a flying object was made by any military personnel on that date. Although hundreds of civilians on foot could not observe the objects from the vicinity (the sighting occurred between the end of the normal working day), no other reports were received on the sighting.

COMMENT BY PREPARING OFFICER:

1. The objects were allegedly hovering in an area located between John Rodgers (Honolulu International Airport) and Hickam Air Force Base. No report of unidentified hovering objects by military and civilian aircraft. Neither military nor civilian control towers sighted any strange objects in the area.

2. The observer is a telephone repair man by trade and has been living in the vicinity of Hickam Air Force Base for a period of 10 years. His knowledge of aircraft is quite extensive.

Col [redacted] his wife and two children were at the Family Drive-In
Theater, 2 miles north of Champaign, Illinois on the evening of 15
August 1952. At 2132 hrs CDT Col [redacted] noticed an especially
bright object in the sky to the west of him at approximately 270
degrees compass heading. When first observed the object was
about 70 to 75 degrees above the horizon. The object moved in a
perpendicular line to a point approximately 40 degrees above the
horizon; then made an abrupt right angle turn to the north and
moved in a horizontal plane to approximately 340 degrees heading
before abruptly disappearing. The object was similar in color to
the green of a welding torch flame and appeared to be several
times larger in diameter than the average star. The object was
round without any apparent tail. It remained in sight four (4)
or five (5) seconds. There was no sound. The weather was clear
with no apparent clouds in the immediate vicinity.

A check at Base Operations, CHANUTE AFB revealed that 2 B-25a

during the pertinent period. The crews of the B-25a observed nothing
unusual during the evening.
