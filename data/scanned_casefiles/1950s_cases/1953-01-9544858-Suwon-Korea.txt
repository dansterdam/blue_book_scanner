1) HISTORY OF INCIDENT PATTERN: The classification here is based on the pattern
of sightings reported in the area. If the sightings follow a definite pattern,
suggesting intelligent control of the objects observed, the incident is classi-
fied as well. If no pattern is observed, the incident is classified as to the
type of object sighted. If multiple sightings are reported over a given area
or time period and resemble each other, they may warrant together a different
classification than they would individually. During the period from 17 to 29 January,
1951, there were several sightings in the Tokyo area, all of which were classi-
fied as well as individually. In the instance where sightings are reported over
an extended area of several hundred miles, in more than one country, all within
a limited time period, such sightings should be given careful study as to whether
they represent a coordinated surveillance pattern.

January 1953 have increased in frequency. It is recommended that close liaison be
maintained with all U.S. Military units in the Far East to insure that all sightings
are reported promptly and that careful investigation is conducted at the point of
observation. Until the time that the sightings can be definitely established as
known or identified phenomena, they should be considered as incidents of possible
intelligence value.

large area of sightings based on the locations indicated. No really
definite geographical pattern can be determined which would indicate
either a definite location or direction of movement. Until the data
can be compiled and analyzed further, it would be impossible to determine
if there is a discernible pattern. The pattern might indicate some activity
in the general area, or it might indicate that reports are sporadic in this
area. It is also to be noted that at least several witnesses in these
gen'l. areas might be aware of previous sightings in the area.

1. On 21 January 1953, 1600Z, in the vicinity of K-13, Suwon, Korea
(37°15'N 127°00'E), source observed two (2) unidentified flying objects
for a period of three (3) to five (5) seconds.

2. Source stated objects were round, approximately twelve (12) to
fourteen (14) inches in diameter, glowing a whitish silver and flying in trail
in a gentle climb. These disks appeared to the observer to be convex, with a
dark line or shadow transversing the base. Source could not estimate speed
but stated objects were moving considerably faster than B-26 or other aircraft
which he has seen daily. No propulsion features, trails, exhausts or sounds
were noted. Source stated objects disappeared either into or behind clouds.

3. Manner of observation was visual from ground. Source estimated
objects were about four (4) miles from him, altitude unknown, flying in a
straight line from West to East.

4. At time of sighting, source was working at the railroad at K-13 and
was facing northeast. Objects were at an elevation of approximately 45 degrees
from observer. One element of two (2) F-86's were in the flight pattern to the
right of the observer and another aircraft of unknown type was on his left
at low altitude.

5. Source is unknown to reporting officer. On second interrogation the
day following his initial report source altered his description of the objects.
His initial statement of speed, distance and alleged time of observation are estimated
to be unreliable. No other witnesses to this observation are known.
