1. An object was sighted by two observers. It was described by one of them, Major R.L. Wallander, as a round blue light with an orange glow emitting low. The other observer, A/3 Phipps, described the object as an orange colored and spreading cloud obscuring about three feet (?) in diameter and varying in shape. After watching the object a few moments Major Wallander sent Airmen Phipps for details. On this occasion Captain - might watch the object. The object was sighted by A/30 Phipps at an estimated (his estimate) 1,200 feet and about 40° elevation above of the runway. It opened in rapid forward motion gaining about 40° in altitude with each sweep and pausing at the top of each sweep for 10-15 seconds. A/30 Phipps states that on each leg the orange glow was shining and behind the object like a comet's tail. The object disappeared toward the NE after about 5 minutes.

Major Wallander, who was standing nearby, apparently saw the object at a slightly later time. When he sighted it, the object appeared to be hovering over the Wichita Municipal Airport building on a bearing of approximately 270° from his position and approximately a mile away. Major Wallander reports that the object was at an elevation of about 20° from his position (south) at the time. It passed over the city of Wichita and then hovered for approximately two minutes. At this time the elevation of the object was approximately 40° with respect to the observer and the bearing had changed from 270° to 350° approximately. It hovered here for approximately two minutes, then moved to a position due north of Wichita and approximately 350° with respect to the observer and traveled out of sight on an approximate course of 350° and rising.

Captain Bellaman arrived on the scene at the beginning of the last phase. He states that he saw an orange light moving north at what appeared to be great speed. When Captain Bellaman arrived the light was at an elevation of 50° above the horizontal.

2. The sighting occurred at approximately 1902Z5 [illegible] and the object was under observation 5-7 minutes. In the T/A this was reported as 1302151 Mow, but it has since been established that Major Wallander's watch was 13 minutes slow.

3. The manner of observation was visual.

12. This report is considered significant because of the reliability and experience of Major Hartanft and Captain Wheaton, and also because the trailing smoke glow may indicate a method of observation. The weather balloons sighted in this report were released at about the time of the observation, and, as will be seen from the listed window would only described a path similar to the one observed. The light attached to each balloon and swinging at the end of a 3' cord might account for the jerky movement described by A/3 Palmer. Oddly Behaving Meteor Flurry
Puzzles Experts in Southwest

By The Associated Press

ALBUQUERQUE—N. M., Nov 9.—AS unusual number of un-usual meteors flashing through southwestern skies these autumn nights has the experts puzzled.

In the last eleven days, seven fireballs of exceptional size have ripped through the skies over a seven-state area. The Institute of Meteoritics at New Mexico University said that frequency has "never been matched before."

"In fact," said Dr. Lincoln La Paz head of the institute, "there has never been a rate of meteorite fall in history that has been one-fifth as high as the present fall. If that rate should continue, I would suspect the phenomenon is not natural."

The fireballs reported by observers in Arizona, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Texas, Colorado and Nevada do not behave or perform like ordinary meteors, he said. They travel in straight lines when they should follow a curved course. Frequently they are silent, although meteors of that size should make a definite noise. They have a greenish color, he said, that has not been reported outside the Southwest.

One of the most unusual was the one that swept 128 miles in a straight line across the sky and has been also in New Mexico, Utah and Nevada, the fireball vanished in a blinding flash somewhere in northern Arizona. There was no noise.

"The most conventional was the one that struck near Oklahoma City Wednesday after being seen in New Mexico, Texas and Colorado. Although it was of the greenish color, it made a noise. Dr. La Paz said he hopes ground

parties will recover fragments of it. He noted that pieces are recovered from only one in ten large meteorites.

Yesterday's show in southern Arizona and New Mexico was described as perhaps the oddest of the series. Dr. La Paz said two objects, flying parallel, crashed near Cloverdale, N. M., in the southwest corner of the state, sending up a double mushroom of smoke or dust.

"Such a case of two meteorites falling together without noise would be hard to duplicate in history," he said. "I don't know what to make of it. I'm almost inclined to ask those fellows out in Nevada (where atomic bomb tests have been held recently) what they are doing."

At Washington, the Defense Department said if the objects might be rockets or guided missiles fired from test areas at White Sands, N.M., or elsewhere. A spokesman replied the department knew nothing of any tests in the Southwest at the time the flying objects were seen.

Light Seen in N.Y. Sky

MIDDLETOWN, N. Y., Nov 9—(AP)—Scores of New Yorkers reported seeing a strange light streaking across the sky late this afternoon. Reports described it variously as "a ball of fire," a "comet" and a "pink streak" high in the sky that took a few minutes to pass from east to west.

WE FACE A MYSTERY of outer space even more arresting than the unsolved "flying saucers."

It is the mystery of the giant green fireballs.

Nine of them flashed across the skies of the Southwestern U.S. late last year. Each vanished as though its light had been switched off. No trace of a single one has been found.

People here are used to "mysteries." They live next door to the super-secret atomic energy plant at Los Alamos. At the edge of town is the armed forces' Sandia Base, where the atom bomb is assembled. Across the state is the rocket laboratory of White Sands. They know what technical wonders our scientists can produce.

Sober scientific observers are beginning to think that the answer to the fireball riddle may mark a milestone in our knowledge of outer space.
