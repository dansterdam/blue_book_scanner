A. Object Data

(1) Shape: Shallow, bell-shaped. (See attachment #1).

(2) Size: The size of this object could not be accurately judged because it was at an unknown distance from the observer's position. The observer stated that the object appeared to be roughly triangular in shape. At one length the subject was approximately 5 1/2 to 5 inches across the base of the triangle and about 6 1/2 inches from apex to base. The object appeared to emit a trail about 3 to 10 times longer than the object itself. She did state that this trail could have been similar to the after-glow of a cigarette moved quickly through the air in a darkened room rather than an actual emitted trail.

(3) Color: The object appeared to the observer to be very bright and colorless. She stated that the "trail" was whiter in color though duller in intensity than the object itself. Under continued observation would give no color impression of the object. The observer stated that the trail appeared to be non-solid and streaked. She did not notice the light reflected in objects within her range of vision nor did she notice reflected light in her darkened room.

(4) Number: One

(5) Formation: N/A

(6) Features: No apparent flight control surfaces.

(7) Trail: (See (3) above.

(8) Sound: None

(9) Other features: None.

B. Description of Course:

(1) What first called observers attention to object?
Mrs _____ [redacted] had risen approximately 10 00152 March 1959 to give her sick child some medicine. At 00252 after having again retired, she was lying in bed observing the stars through her bedroom window which faces north, when the object appeared at the left or eastern edge of her bedroom window.

(2) Angle or elevation and azimuth when first seen:
Mrs _____ stated that when first seen the object was about 15° above the horizon. From her position, its distance from the windows and her description she first observed the object about 170 to 175° azimuth.

(3) Angle of elevation and azimuth when last seen:
When last seen the object was between 20° and 25° above the horizon and at 220° - 225° azimuth from observers position.

(4) Flight Path:
The object was observed south of 34° 34' 30"N/11l°0' 22' 15" W at an unknown distance traveling in a straight line shallow ascent from east to west. The observer stated that the objects line of flight could have been East-Southeast to West-Northwest.

K. Position, title, and comments of preparing officer:

(2) Comments:

(a) Mrs. [redacted] had heard of Unidentified Flying Objects and knew vaguely that the Air Force was interested in these reports.

(b) She had never before seen anything like this object. She was absolutely certain that this object was not an airplane and gas as certain that it was not a meteorite. The objects speed was too fast and silent to be an airplane and too slow to be that of a meteorite.

(c) Mrs. [redacted] appeared to be sincere in her effort to report factually and was convinced that what she saw does exist.

(d) She denied wearing glasses and stated that upon last examination her eyes were normal.

(e) 1 There were no other reports of UFOs at or near the time of Mrs. [redacted] observation.

2 Personnel in the George AFB Air Traffic Control Tower, 2½ miles north of observers position, saw nothing unusual that night and had no air traffic in their system for about two hours before or three hours after the sighting time.

3 The tower personnel reported no astronomical or meteorological phenomena that could account for this sighting.

(f) Mrs. [redacted] seemed to be a lucid, honest person. She firmly believes she saw what she reported and reported that she saw.

(g) There is no evidence, pro or con, other than Mrs. [redacted] word to substantiate this report.

l. Existance of physical evidence: none.
