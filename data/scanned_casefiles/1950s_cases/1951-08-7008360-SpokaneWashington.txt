SUBJECT: Report of Information on Unconventional Aircraft

TO:      Commanding General
         Air Material Command
         ATTN: MCTS    ATIC
         Wright-Patterson Air Force Base
         Dayton, Ohio

1. In accordance with the provisions of paragraphs 4 and 5, Air Defense Command Letter 200-1, dated 11 April 1951, subject as above, the following report of an unconventional aircraft or object, which was sighted in this area, is forwarded for your information.

   a. Description- The object was elliptical and appeared to be flat in appearance. Observer judged object to be about two (2) feet long and 8" to 10" in width. Color was white or aluminum. No trail or exhaust was noticed. If object emitted any noise, same was faint and to a busy highway very near to point of observation. Man direct sun the object was 50 to 75 feet in the air going straight up slowly. The point of origin seemed to be almost directly from the bottom of a hill and approximately 1/4 mile from observer. When the object reached a height equal to or slightly greater than the top of the 300-400 foot hill, it changed direction to the south and started a 30-40 degree climb. The movement at all times was very steady and even, no jerky motions, no vibration or fluctuations being noticed. The object continued in a steady 30-40 degree climb to south until out of sight. Witness judged object about two (2) miles away when disappeared from sight and had appearance then of a star, probably due to sun reflecting on the bright surface of object.

   b. Object sighted approximately 1110P, 20 August 1951. Duration of sight was approximately 20 minutes.

   c. The object was sighted with the naked eye from the ground only. Speed was very slow and observer unable to judge height when object disappeared from view. Observer stated no aircraft were seen during this observation.

d. The point of observation was 47 degrees 23' N and 117 degrees 24' E in reference to a known landmark, approximately 1/4 mile south of the junction of Washington State Highway 195 with Washington State Highway 31. This location is also approximately 7 1/2 miles 23 of Fairchild and on airways (Blue 1). The object was first seen in pursuant to the observer a 5T 1/4 miles from observation point.

e. Neither observer professed much knowledge of aircraft stating that neither one had even ever been up in an airplane; however, due to their proximity to Geiger Field, they stated they could readily identify various types of aircraft that every day flow over this area.

f. 1130P CEG O50 169/92/47-9/007

g. In the opinion of the station weather officer, neither the convective activity nor the synoptic situation over the area could likely have caused an object this size to float or ascend at time of incident. The air was dry and stable, no clouds whatsoever being in the area at the time. The witnesses stated that at the point of observation the wind was calm as the clothes on the line were not even fluttering.

h. A thorough inspection of the area from which the object apparently arose was made on foot. No physical evidence or fragments were found.

i. None.

j. Aircraft airborne in local area at the time of observation:

The Navy Operations at Geiger Field reported three Navy P2V's that might have been in the area at the time. The P2V's each landed at Geiger at separate times, the first at 1201P and the last at 1223P. They filed from Seattle, being airborne sufficient time to do early local flying in this area. Spokane radio reported the arrival of three aircraft at Palis Field, two Cessna landing at there, one Stinson arriving at 1150P, all arriving from Boise via Airways (Blue 1) and all under 5000'. There were no aircraft from Geiger Field airborne at 0...of observation.
