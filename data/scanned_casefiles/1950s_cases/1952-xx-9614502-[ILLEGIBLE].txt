SUMMARY: On 12 June, 1952, [redacted] the source of this report, was
standing at the passenger entrance gate south of the Municipal Airport Terminal.
At 1610, he observed an object which was then overdue. He noticed in the east a round white object hovering in the sky. The object remained almost stationary for 10-15 seconds, then moved southwest at a fast rate
of speed. The object stopped, made several maneuvers, and navigated in a field until
it reached its original position, stopped and took off directly east at an estimated
speed of 200 miles per hour. At this time the object appeared to change from a
globular to a saucer shape. Mr. [redacted], the second observer says that the shape
was not discernible, but said the object appeared to glint like metal. He describes
numerous reversals of direction and says the object was moving faster than T-33's
flying in the immediate vicinity of the observers.

IR-59-52          2          2

Description of Object: Color white - round globular appearing object. Diameter approximately 30 feet. Object hovered for about a period of 10 to 15 seconds. Suddenly object moved across the sky at a fairly fast rate of speed in a south-westerly direction. It stopped suddenly, hovered for a moment, then went thru a series of maneuvers, gained altitude, flashed around and then drew back into its original point where first sighted. No noise, trails, or exhaust were apparent to the observer. Impression of speed 400 miles per hour or faster. Altitude 500 feet, distance no three miles.

Time of Sighting: Object sighted at 1610. Entire observation took about 30 to 40 seconds.

Manner of Observation: Visually from the ground. No optical or electronic equipment used.

Location of Observer: The observers were located at 37°13'3" - 97°16'4". Altitude of object approximately 500 feet, distance 2 to 3 miles and headed in a southwesterly direction.

Identifying Information of Observer: One of the observers is associated with the Beech Aircraft Corporation, in the training Department. The second observer is the Bell Aircraft Corporation's Resident Representative at the Boeing Airplane Company, Wichita, Kansas.

Atmospheric Conditions: Sky clear, no cloud formations, wind velocity 25 miles per hour from southwest (observer checked this by car radio which was close at hand)

Meteorological Conditions: None

Evidence of Physical Evidence: None

Interception or Identification Action Taken: None

Location of Air Traffic in General Area: Two T-33 aircraft were noted by the observers, one flying north, and another some distance away in the northern part of the sky, but neither near the object.

This report was not sent by electrical means since too much time had elapsed between the sighting and the receipt of the report by this office.

COMMENTS of Preparing Officer:

The local Weather Bureau reported at 1612 that cloud cover was scattered with tops at 18,000 feet, wind 350 at 24 knots with gusts to 29 knots, altimeter 0777, temperature 91°, dew point 70. Both observers are employed in the aircraft industry. It seems probable that they did see some airborne object which behaved erratically, but which could have been a T-33 practicing aerobatics, although it is the opinion of both observers that the maneuvers were much too abrupt for a T-33.

STATEMENT

I was called from my Hotel room early Wednesday morning, 30 July (Approximately Central Standard Time 0035) by Lt. Albright. He stated that he was interviewing a man who not only had seen a flying object take off but had examined it on the ground. He asked that I join him at the Filter Center to hear the man's fantastic tale. When I arrived at the Filter Center the man immediately engaged me in conversation. While being noncommittal in my replies I made the following observations.

Mr. [redacted] is a man of middle age, of medium height, dark brown hair and had a deep suntanned complexion. He was more earnest than excited in his conversation and spoke with a slight German accent. He answered in German when I queried him in that language. He said that he had recently left a good job in California to get away from his wife and did not want her to know where he was now. He was cautioned regarding the penalty of falsifying on official statements. He didn't display fear in his respect and stated that he had no reason for telling anything but the truth, that he did not expect anything out of it, and only wanted to get the subject off of his mind and to the proper authorities. Observed that his left index finger was missing and that the hearing of his left ear was impaired by a fire cracker explosion.

I followed through on the interrogation made by Lt. Albright and the Operations Officer of the 138th AO & J Squadron who gathered the information by phone for the telegraph report of this nature required by regulations. I was impressed through out the period with his memory and detailed description of the object. He never wavered or changed his story on any part of the subject. The following items did weaken my belief in his story:

a.    The fact that he had waited a week before disclosing the story.

b.    Why would he mention that he had a camera in his car and not think to use it?

c.    The loss of the stamp that he thought came from the crew's cigarette package.

d.    His story about his wife and his reluctance for any publicity.

1.  DESCRIPTION OF OBJECTS:

a.  ELECTRONIC SIGHTINGS: Numerous unidentified flying objects of undetermined size and shape were sighted by Ground Electronic means in the early morning of 25 July 1952, by members of the 6704a ACM Squadron, Osceola, Wisconsin, who were then on duty. Two of the sightings were positively identified with the exception of some sightings which appeared as an arc (reference is made to Fax #2 this report). Area would appear and fade on the radar scope as a complete unit got one push of the air appearing and fading before another push. There was no indication of any organized formation noted during sightings. Speeds varied between high700 and 20,000 mph as recorded on the range scope set on the 200 nautical. mile, Targets appearing on the radar scope followed a general pattern of a clockwise cycle, running southeast then westerly, and finally north easterly. All cycle would take approximately two (2) minutes and twenty-five (25) seconds for completion. There was an average delay of one (1) minute between cycles. This represented an area and again during the entire sighting period. Toward the end of the sightings it was extremely difficult to pick out any one (1) cycle due to the heavy sporadic sightings in the northern half of the radar scope and the lessening of the defined sightings in the southern half of the radar scope. There was some correlation between these electronic sightings and visual sightings made by pilots of the 129th Fighter Squadron, held-down in Chamberlain Field, Minneapolis, who were scrambled by the 6704a ACM Squadron in conjunction with the radar sightings. This correlation placed both types of sightings in the same area at the same time.

b.  VISUAL SIGHTINGS: Numerous unidentified flying lights were visually observed in the early morning of 25 July 1952, by members of the 6704a ACM Squadron, Osceola, Wisconsin, who were then on duty. The lights observed were approximately one (1) second with three (3) to six (5) seconds elapsing between flashes. The flashes moved very rapidly across the sky. One (1) sighting was of two (2) simultaneous flashes in parallel movement which was the only indication of any possible formation noted. Size and speed were not determined due to lack of comparison with known objects. Lights progressed from the eastern horizon to the western horizon south of the site of the 6704a ACM Squadron, at that was judged to be twenty(20) degrees above the horizon.

2.  TIME OF SIGHTINGS:

a.  ELECTRONIC SIGHTINGS: Sightings occurred between 2501228 and 2502158 lasting one (1) hour and twenty-three (23) minutes.

b.  VISUAL SIGHTINGS: All visual sightings covered by this report occurred between 2501358 and 2502003 lasting thirty (30) minutes.
