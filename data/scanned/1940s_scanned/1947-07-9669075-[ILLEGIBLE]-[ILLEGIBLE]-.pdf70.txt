STATEMENT FOR THE OFFICER IN CHARGE:

Mr. [REDACTED] Berkeley, California, was interviewed 9 September 1947 at his place of employment, the X-ray wing building, University of California, concerning a report made to have seen on 29 July 1947 while with Captain [REDACTED] of the U.S.N. [U?]. Mr. [REDACTED] stated in substance that he was a lot [?] on the [REDACTED] and that on the 29th of July 1947 shortly after 12 noon he and [REDACTED] had just landed from a routine training flight when Captain [REDACTED] called Mr. [REDACTED] attention to an unidentified flying object. Mr. [REDACTED] was following a P-80 aircraft. Mr. [REDACTED] sighted the J-30 to be flying at approximately 250 miles per hour on a preliminary approach to landing at Hamilton Field. In Mr. [REDACTED] estimation of the object he saw following the J-30 maintained a speed of three to four times that of the aircraft. A moment later a second object appeared and flew a similar parallel to a number paralleling a man accompanying a nearer ships, or a left to right movement, over the object first sighted. Mr. [REDACTED] stated that his observation continued until the objects were out of sight. He estimated the time to be approximately 15 seconds, the course approximately 120 degrees. Mr. [REDACTED] could not estimate the size of the objects nor actual altitude, though he did not believe them to be beyond his normal range of foot. Mr. [REDACTED] described the objects as being silky white in color, and unlike any conventional type aircraft he had ever seen. When questioned whether he was familiar with a Navy "Firebat", Mr. [REDACTED] stated that he was, and that he was certain the objects he reported seeing were not of this nature.
