A topographic survey of Alaska has been in progress since 1893, and nearly 44 percent of its area has now been mapped. About 15 percent of the Territory has been covered by maps on a scale of 1/250,000 (1 inch = 8 miles). For most of the remainder of the area surveyed the maps published are on a scale of 1/62,500 (1 inch = nearly 1 mile). For some areas of particular economic importance, covering about 4,300 square miles, the maps published are on a scale of 1/31,250 (1 inch = nearly 1 mile) or larger. In addition to the area covered by topographic maps, about 11,300 square miles of southeastern Alaska has been covered by planimetric maps on scales of 1/125,000 and 1/63,360.

The Hawaiian Islands have been surveyed, and the resulting maps are published on a scale of 1/62,500.
