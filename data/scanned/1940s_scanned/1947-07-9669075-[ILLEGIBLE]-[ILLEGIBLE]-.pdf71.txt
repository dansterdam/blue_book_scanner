MEMORANDUM FOR THE OFFICER IN CHARGE:

On 5 August 1947, this agent interviewed Captain [redacted]
[redacted] the [redacted] r in the 415th AAF SU,
Hamilton Field, California, regarding flying objects in the
air. Captain [redacted] stated in substance: He is an instrument pilot in
the 415th Training Unit. On 29 July at 1430, Captain [redacted] and a student,
1st Lt. [redacted] California, landed
and walked an AT-6 near the hangar, building 59 of the 415th AAF SU.
Walking from the aircraft toward reserve operations, and looking westward
just above the hangar (approximately 10 miles distant) saw a P-80 flying westward toward
Oakland. Above this P-80, at approximately 8,000 to 10,000 feet in altitude, Captain [redacted] saw two flying objects traveling the same direction
as the P-80. He judged the solid, white, somewhat shiny objects
were approximately 18 to 25 feet in diameter. [redacted] stated that he
didn't call them discs due to the publicity they have been given,
but in shape they were circular, like a ball on the bottom, but not completely round; and [redacted] specifically stated that these objects were not
airplanes.

The first flying object was traveling straight and level; whereas
the second was close behind the first, same altitude, only 15 percent to
be going from left to right and right to left, more or less like a guard
in an aircraft formation. Captain [redacted] further stated that the speed
of these two objects made a P-30 look as if it was motionless in the air.

The sky was clear, consequently Captain [redacted] and Lt. [redacted] watched
the two objects until they disappeared in flight, supposedly over Oakland,
headed toward the ocean.

Due to the noise of the P-80, no sound of any wind could be heard from
these flying objects.

These objects did not leave any vapor, smoke, or any visible trails
along their flight path.

Captain [redacted] informed this agent he had never told anyone about
these flying objects, fearing that people would think he was crazy.

AGENT'S NOTES: Captain [redacted] showed extreme sincerity throughout the
interview, answering all questions with similar assurance.

JAMES A. NELSON, Spec. Agt., CIC AAF
