9. The Honolulu Police Department advised on 1 October 1949, that during the afternoon and early evening hours of 30 September 1949, no report had been received by them of any unidentified flying object.

10. 1st Lt NORMAN H. STEVENS, AO 821899 and Cpl JAMES R. OLSON, AF 11 176 125, Operations Officer and Operations Clerk, respectively, 1500th Air Base Group, Hickam Air Force Base, advised, on 2 October 1949, that there was little flying aircraft activity at vicinity of Hickam Air Force Base at about 1700 hours. Flights on the day usually land at about 1530 hours while night flight schedules do not begin before 1900 hours. Examination of log disclosed a C-47, Base Flight Aircraft, inbound from Parting Sands, Saipan, which landed at 1714 hours. An A/D (C-47) assigned to VJ Squadron 352 (USD) inbound from Barbers Point, Oahu, landed at 1725 hours. Two P-47 Aircraft of the Hawaiian National Guard landed at 1654 hours. No sightings were reported. All aircraft described are believed to have been well under 6,000 feet while in flight and were readily identifiable as such.

11. Mr. WILLIAM A. THURGOOD, Meteorological Aide, US Weather Bureau, Honolulu Airport Station, reported on 2 October 1949, that he released a white colored radiosonde balloon at 1636 hours, local time, 30 September 1949, and that he followed the flight of the balloon by means of a theodolite. According to THURGOOD, this balloon traveled in the general northerly arc described by Lts NORMAN and MARTIN at about the times reported. THURGOOD confirmed report of Mr. WOLFF as to general description of the balloon; that these balloons expand while rising, and that they can be observed with the naked eye up to 45,000 feet. If followed closely and atmospheric conditions are favorable. It was THURGOOD's opinion that atmospheric conditions were favorable during the afternoon of 30 September. THURGOOD also reported that the balloons used by the local Weather Station are in three (3) colors - white, for use on clear days, black for use on cloudy days, yellow for use in haze - and that the white balloon had been released on 30 September because of its properties in reflecting the sunlight. THURGOOD advised that while "tracking" this balloon, he observed it to shift to and fro due to wind action, but did not observe it to spin, nor did he observe any condition which could account for report of smoke or an exhaust. THURGOOD did offer the observation that perhaps eye strain and optical illusion could account for such report, recalling that prior game of gazing steadily at a spot, shifting the eyes and the spot watched would seem to move.
