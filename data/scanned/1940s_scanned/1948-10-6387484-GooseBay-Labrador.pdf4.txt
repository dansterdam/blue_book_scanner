Hq, 1297th Air Base Group, Newfoundland Base Command, MATS,
APO 877, c/o Postmaster, New York, N.Y. 24 January 1949.

TO: CC, Air Materiel Command, Wright-Patterson Air Force
Base, Dayton, Ohio. ATTENTION: MCIAXX-3.

1. In compliance with basic letter, the attached reports are submitted:

2. Message 9816CCZ contained three (3) sightings, which were handled separately and are listed as Inclosures #1, #2
and #3. Message C1130Z is listed as Inclosure #4. There
are no records in this office, or at this station, of a
message 0154Z. It is believed that this number is in
error. Our message C#151CZ, which was initiated by the
1329th Air Co. and relayed to the Headquarters, probably delayed in Message Center and the data time group
changed to U#2260Z. This message referred to a sighting
at 2145Z on 1 November 1948.

3. Our message 0915J3Z, referred to above, could not
be plotted on a sketch of the TTT scope or the base map
because:

a. By plotting a point three and three quarter
(3 3/4) miles from the station on a bearing of one hundred
and twenty-three degrees (123°) and following a true path
on a bearing of two hundred and forty-nine degrees (249°)
for a distance of seven and one half (7 1/2) miles where the
message states the object disappeared, would not put the
object's disappearing point one hundred and ninety-two degrees (182°) from the station.

b. This same message stated that the object traveled the distance of four (4) miles in four (4) minutes
and seven (7) seconds timed by a stop watch. If this were
true, the object could not have disappeared from Radar and
could still have had a time lapse of eight and one half
(8 1/2) miles before it disappeared at one hundred and eighty-two
(182°) degrees from the station, assuming that it did travel
on a bearing of two hundred and forty-nine degrees (249°) as
stated.
