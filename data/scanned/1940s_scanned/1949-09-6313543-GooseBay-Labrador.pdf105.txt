"NOTAM KHNL 210102Z02

HONOLULU AIRPORT. BALLOON WITH RADIOSONDE ATTACHED WILL BE RELEASED FROM
HONOLULU AIRPORT AT 1635H. EXPECTED TO REACH 10000 FEET AT 1545H"

6. Mr. ROBERT L. WOLFF, Meteorological Aide, US Weather Bureau, Honolulu
Airport Station, reported, 30 September 1949, that a radiosonde balloon had
been released by that facility at 1635 hours, Hawaiian time, 30 September 1949
(0235 Z October 29) and was in flight for forty-eight (48) minutes. Flight
terminating by burst of balloon. WOLFF reported that the balloon involved was a
helium filled, white color, pure gum rubber sphere which was inflated to a diameter of 7 feet. Suspended 50 feet from the balloon was a radiosonde transmitter
in a box approximately 4" x 10" x 10". Attached to the radiosonde transmitter
was an aerial three (3)-feet in length. Midway between the transmitter and
balloon was a paper parachute, shifi (3) feet in diameter. On conclusion with
the balloon burst, the parachute permitted the transmitter to glide, rather
than plummet, to the earth. The balloon used on this date was white in color,
so selected because of the clear day and the color reflecting the sunlight, enabling the night to be followed with ease through an optical instrument such
as the theodolite. Three (3) colors of balloons are employed by the local Weather
Bureau according to WOLFF, white for use on the bright clear day; yellow for use
in haze and black for use on a cloudy day. Rate of ascent of balloon is dependant on
winds; however, the average rate is roughly 1000 feet per minute. As the balloon rises,
it expands in atmospheric pressure, reaching diameter of from 12 to 18 feet, according to WOLFF. WOLFF further advises that if atmospheric conditions are favorable,
it is possible to pick out and follow a balloon at a height of 45,000 feet. After
consulting a Winds Aloft Computation Sheet prepared from the flight of this balloon,
Mr. WOLFF expressed the opinion that the unidentified flying object observed could
well have been the balloon released by the Weather Bureau. On this balloon was in
flight during the time and followed the path described. Mr. WOLFF further advised
that the balloon was tracked by Mr. A. THRAWOOD, Meteorological Aide, US
Weather Bureau, Honolulu Airport Station.
