'Saucer' Soars in

8 Spot 'Saucer'
Disc Leaves
S.L. Pilot
After Chase

By Darlene Galbreath
Tribune Correspondent

What keeps a routine
flight Monday noon into a
Lake City pilot turned into an
exciting aerial adventure was
an investigation by Air Force
officials into a report of a
"flying saucer."

REPORT MADE BY J. Harris,
631 Garfield Ave. (USN S.
State), was the first to sight an
unidentified object. He said he
was preparing for takeoff on a
flight from Hill Air Command
port, 3200 W. 2100 South. At
that seven o'clock Monday
morning, he spotted an object on
the ground.

Mr. Harris, a Salt Lake real
estate man and licensed pilot,
and veteran Air Force official
that he first sighted what he
thought was another plane when
he turned onto the airstrip
and immediately began his
takeoff roll.

"AT FIRST I thought it was
another plane south of the
field," Mr. Harris said, "but
when I was airborne I was surprised to find the object was
still in the same location as
when I had turned on to it."

IT WAS THEN, Mr. Harris
reported, that he decided to
investigate.

"I changed my heading," Mr.
Harris continued, "and flew
into what appeared to be a
shallow bank and spot ted what
a rocking motion at an altitude of between 8,500 and 7,000
feet."

THE PILOT radioed back
to Utah Central Airport that
he had sighted what is
thought to be a "flying saucer"
and asked for an attempt to
get a closer view.

Mr. Harris told officials that
he estimated the distance between his plane and the object
at approximately 2,000 feet at
the time of the sighting.

HE THEN REPORTED a posi-
tion about three miles from it
when he suddenly moved up
ward like an elevator and be-
gan to move north.

The unidentified object, he reported, showed it to
be a light gray disc white he
could not determine the altitude
or locate same and about four
feet across at the widest point.

"THERE WERE NO opening visible, no vapor trail or exhaust smoke, yet I am sure
it was a controlled craft from
the performance during my
observation," Mr. Harris concluded.

The pilot followed the "saucer" when it began its flight
and then was able to observe it
again when it suddenly halted
its northward movement and
moved almost directly over the Utah
Lake Omni station.

"AFTER HOVERING there,"
Mr. Harris said, "it moved
straight up. Then began to
move level at an extremely
high speed and then vanished.
disappeared within a few seconds."

During his chase, Mr.
Harris maintained radio contact with both the Utah Central Airport and the Provo
Airport.

He relayed all available
information to the FAA communications tower at the Salt
Lake Municipal Airport.

OTHER WITNESSES, some
of whom used binoculars to
watch the object, were Mr. and
Mrs. Jay Galbraith, 3264400
South; Roy Redmond, near

201 E. 700 North, Provo, an
airport employee.

ALL OF THE witnesses reported watching the object for
varying lengths of time.

Mr. Redmond reported that
"whatever it was seemed to be
coming while hovering almost
stationary just south of the
airport," and "at one time
it almost looked like a zeppelin.

"WE PASSED the field
glasses around," Mr. Redmond
continued, "and all of us had
a good view."

All the witnesses agreed that
when the object moved, it was
at a terrific rate of speed.
