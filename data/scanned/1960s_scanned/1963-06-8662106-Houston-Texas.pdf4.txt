[stamp: 1527 CHAMBER OF COMMERCE BUILDING
HOUSTON 5, TEXAS]

June 14, 1963

Commander Major Kolenda
OST Detachment 1004
Ellington Air Force Base
Houston, Texas


Gentlemen:

I am an american male, age thirty five years, residing in Houston, Harris County, Texas. I am a graduate Civil Engineer and a duly licensed and practicing attorney-at-law.

On the 14th day of June, 1963 at 9:37 p.m. I, accompanied by my wife, was observing the constellations from a point on top of the Addicks Dam near the intersection of Jack Rabbit Road and the Katy Highway (Harris County, Texas); which point is roughly one and eight tenths miles west of the city limits of Houston.

At this time and place I observed what appeared to be a star situated approximately ten degrees directly below Polaris. This object was the same apparent diameter as Polaris and shone with a light slightly more intense.

At about this time the object began to move from point 1 (exhibit A) to point 2 shown thereon. This phenomena was also observed by my wife and her sister as it is traced as exhibit B. Both sketches were made less than one hour after our observations and without reference to the other.

We estimate the total elapsed time from observing the object's first motion (pt 1) to its disappearance at point 2 to be roughly 38 minutes.

As regards motion: The object moved rather generally, but much more slowly than the velocity associated with a meteor. It apparently stopped twice, at points A and B on the diagrams for periods of approximately twenty seconds without any corresponding loss of intensity during its arrested motion.
