Dear Lieutenant-Colonel Spaulding:

Thank you for your letter of July 16, regarding the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP). I agree with the NICAP that UFO's are "a problem of national importance" -- maybe -- and for that reason, I am very glad the Air Force checks on them.

However, as you will see from the enclosed, which I send in case it may be of interest to you, there would seem to be some Fayettevilliana who regard these flying objects, identified or unidentified, as of interstellar or cosmic importance. The Keyhoe outfit, of course. Mr. Ted Wylie, our local editor, is either more naive than one would think, or having a lot of fun with these people. I saw his your letter, and the first item is his follow-up. Later, a meeting of some local and Arkansas members of the group was covered by a reporter from the Times, with considerable publicity--and the usual fantastic reactions.

The only UFO's I have seen around these parts are (1) the tail light of a high-flying plane, U by/me alone; and (2) a strange light, hovering on the western horizon after sunset, blinking on and off a phosphorescent light, like a magnesium flare -- until I identified it as a lightning bug perched on the nearest twig of the willow tree by the back porch!

Incidentally, my letter to the NICAP, addressed to Matawan, New Jersey (the best address [redacted] could remember) was returned -- "Insufficient Address" -- "Unknown".

Sincerely,

[handwritten: Robert Hume Campbell]
