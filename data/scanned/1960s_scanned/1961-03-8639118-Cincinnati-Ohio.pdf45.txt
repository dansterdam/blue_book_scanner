APR 25 1961

The report of your sighting of an unidentified flying object in the vicinity of Cincinnati, Ohio, on 2 March 1961 has been analyzed as due to a relatively well-known natural phenomenon. This was not an ordinary meteor, but one of that exceptionally bright class known as "fireballs". Meteors of this type are approximately as bright as Venus at its brightest, of uncommonly long duration, and any display variation colors. These meteors are relatively rare and most people never see one in their lifetime. Due to the rarity of observation of this type of meteor, and their startling appearance, it is not unusual for them to be misidentified.

Your report of the sighting was unique in that it contained sufficient information explained in a manner which contributed immeasurably to the successful analysis.

Thank you for your interest in this matter.

Sincerely

PHILIP G. EVANS
