The following is a summary and narrative of the observing pilot.

On the morning to base from a mission and had crossed the "High Core" at approximately 12,000 feet A and B. I hold the Radar Observer 1 continue south for five (5) minutes, make a procedure turn and return to the High core. After a procedure turn to the right, I held course due east (120) seconds, then started a turn to the left to roll out on three hundred sixty (360) degrees.

Upon reaching a heading of approximately forty-five (45) degrees both the Radar Observer and myself noticed this bright light approximately forty-five (45) degrees to our left and slightly below us. Light could only approach to as time three (-23) inches and it was between us and the Dallas the light appeared to be similar in size and shape stationary any over the vicinity of Cape Bay, Texas.

I attempted to contact the controller on "B" channel but could not raise him. I continued to the base and finally contacted the controller on "B" channel. I reported approximately two (2) miles south and asked him if he had any type over Cape Bay. As I approached the base, the "light" seemed to build up in and increased in size to approximately one and half (1½) inches. The light had build north and then disappeared. The controller finally told me he had two targets moving south at approximately three hundred sixty (350) knots north of Bakerfield tower. This was approximately at 0630 Z.

The "light" was under constant observation from 0549 Z until 0600 Z. The "light" went out once while the "light" was stationary over Cape Bay. This was only momentary. It disappeared momentarily again while driving to the South. The "light" finally faded away at 0600 Z on a downward heading.

Our airborne radar was inoperative and as were low on fuel so were not able to follow. Not anymore accurately than previously stated. The "light" did move from its first position to its stationary position in that time a minute because I was pointed at it while it was moving and did not stay pointed there. I saw this because I checked my High Cone Line, my procedure from there, to a position approximately ten (10) miles north of the Gold at 20,000 feet at the planned lower (210) knots indicated, and on the ground at 0600 Z.

All observations can be confirmed by the radar operator."
