I. SOURCE: [redacted]

Address: [redacted] Dallas, Texas

Age: 23

Occupation: Civil Engineer

Education: One year post graduate work

Qualifications: Civil Engineer

II. RELIABILITY: SOURCE's description of sighting was excellent and in great detail. In own quite familiar with the latest type jet aircraft, astronomy, and weather balloons. SOURCE was very familiar with vector problems, angular distance, and bearing terms. The Officer is at present a Civil Engineer working for a Dallas construction company. Preparing Officer considers the SOURCE to be reliable.

III. SOURCE'S DESCRIPTION OF SIGHTING:

1. On Monday 20 June 1955 at approximately 2200 hours CST, SOURCE was aroused by conversation in front of his home and he stepped outside. SOURCE was soon joined by his neighbor who sighted a rapidly moving light, brighter looking than a first magnitude star. The light traveled at a speed about three times greater than any aircraft he had ever seen. The light made three passes over approximately the same couple. The time of the observation was more than sixty seconds in each sighting with a time lag of about five minutes between sightings. Viewing the light through a light power field glasses, SOURCE stated that the light appeared a three light incandescent white lights with a small red light similar to a wing light on an airplane propeller at either end. All lights were parallel to the flight path of object. This view of the lights appeared only during the second sighting. On the third sighting, thru field glasses, the light appeared as only a single white light. On the first pass, SOURCE only observed a white light, but he was not using the field glasses.

2. SOURCE stated that there was no moon present on the night of the observation, but that there were many stars in the sky.

3. The lights seemed to be at an altitude of seventy-five thousand feet.

4. At no time did the SOURCE state that he had seen an object. All the SOURCE saw was an incandescent light or lights.

5. The edges of the light were similar to that of a bright star.

6. Without field glasses, the light seemed to be about the size of a pin held at arm's length. When the field glasses were used, the light appeared to be about the size of a quarter when held in the hand at arm's length.

7. SOURCE estimated the length of the light to be three hundred feet.

8. Observer first sighted the light in the northwest.
