AIR INTELLIGENCE INFORMATION REPORT

FROM (Armed)                                    REPORT NO.              PAGE _____ OF _____ PAGES
HQ 1706TH DSFWING NANG              4706 D-4-52         1        OF    1

1. One vivid blue round object, the size of a B-29, with short gray red exhaust,
without audible sound, travelled straight NE to NW course at estimated two miles from
observer and passed from sight below horizon.

2. Time: 2330 CDST, 28 June 1952 (0430Z 29 Jun), Duration of sighting: 1½ to
2 minutes.

3. Observation was visual from surface.

4. Observer was several miles north of Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and object appeared
to be two miles east and on a parallel to the coast.

5. Reliability of observer is unknown.

6. Weather at time of sightings: Scattered clouds at 3000 feet and 2000 feet,
visibility 10 miles, wind temperature inversion 19,400 feet to 25,000 feet.
The winds aloft were: S 210/08, 50 220/16, 10 240/2, 15 260/25, 20 280/20,
25 250/14, 30 260/14.

7. Madison newspaper, Wisconsin State Journal, reported a huge meteor, purplish
with yellow tail, crossed from South to Northwest, east of Madison at +225 hours CDST,
28 June. Reported to newspaper by Harold Evans, civilian weather observer.

8. No photographs were known to have been taken.

9. No interception action was taken.

10. Air traffic is unknown.


NEIL M. MacLACHLAN
Captain, USAF
Intelligence Officer
