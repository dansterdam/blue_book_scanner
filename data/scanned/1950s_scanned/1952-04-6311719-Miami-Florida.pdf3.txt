SUMMARY: [illegible text at top portion]

1. On the night of 5 April 1952 at 2115 EST, [redacted] Florida observed four (4) unusual objects glide through the sky around Miami, Florida. He did not make a report promptly, but notified the Airdrome Officer of the 135th Troop Carrier Wing (M) on the following day by telephone. The report was relayed to the Wing Intelligence Officer who visited Mr. Van Deren and obtained his account of the incident as outlined below.

2. Mr. [redacted] and his nine year old son were lying in hammocks in the yard of their home, gazing at the sky. He [redacted] is interested in astronomy, and owns a small telescope, but was not using it at the time. There was a rather heavy haze in the atmosphere at the time, enough to throw a large ring around the moon which was almost full. There were no clouds in the upper half of the sky, only a dark grayish perhaps at the lower edge of the moon. It was followed by a second shape which crossed the upper edge of the moon; a third which passed the lower edge of the moon and a fourth which passed directly across the face of the moon. These objects were circular, dark, completely opaque, and were invisible even when outlined against the moon. The one which passed directly across the moon completed its passage in approximately one (1) second. Its diameter, from the observer's point of view was about half the moon's diameter. That is, superimposed upon the face of the moon, it blocked out about half the moon's span. (See attached free-hand sketch made by Mr. Van Deren). The objects moved from west to east. The leading edge was sharply defined, but a hazy corona surrounded about 200 degrees of its outline, which was perfectly circular. No light nor noise emanated from the objects.

3. Although three (3) of the objects were only seen partially, Mr. [redacted] was sure that they were all the same size and shape. His son also saw one of the objects but any it is an elliptical shape rather than circular. However, it is felt that his father's observation were more accurate, since he is a competent artist and is a paid, interested to noticing shapes.

4. Comment by 135th Wing Intelligence Officer: There is no reason to believe that the observations of Mr. [redacted] were not accurate. He impressed the interviewer as being latter qualified
