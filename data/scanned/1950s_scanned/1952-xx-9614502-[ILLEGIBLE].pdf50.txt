IR-324-52                                2         2

1. One report, night sighting, object sighted visually at approximately 1630EST
19 August 1952, at estimated altitude of 2,000 feet. It was moving south-southwest in
direction, and was described as smaller than a DC-3.* No trail or exhaust was observed.
Object was observed for about fifteen minutes before it disappeared behind the mountains.

2. First report indicated that object was observed in the vicinity of Waterville,
Pennsylvania (Lat 5 = 77.4°W). Supplementary report indicates object was in vicinity
of Avis and Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania.

3. The following persons observed the objects

a. [redacted], U.S. Signal Corps, Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania

b. Mrs.[redacted] Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania

c. Mrs.[redacted], Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania

d. [redacted] Inspector of the B & S Service Station at Avis, Pennsylvania

4. Weather conditions at the time of sighting (1630 EST reports):

a. Williamsport, Pennsylvania - Scattered clouds at 4,500 and 10,000 feet, at
least 15 miles visibility. Winds shift from West-northwest at 30 knots.

b. [redacted] Pennsylvania - Scattered clouds at 5,000 and 12,000 feet, 10
miles visibility. Winds shift from West-northwest at 30 knots.

c. Williamsport, Pennsylvania - Scattered clouds at 5,000 and 8,000 feet, 10
miles visibility. Winds shift from West-northwest at 30 knots.

5. No reported meteorological activity which might account for the sighting. No
physical evidence. No interception attempted. No air traffic reported in the general
area at time of sighting. Angles of observation unknown. Witnesses were not contacted
or interviewed.

6. Original AIRCSRT submitted via electrical means by Olmsted Flight Service
Center.
