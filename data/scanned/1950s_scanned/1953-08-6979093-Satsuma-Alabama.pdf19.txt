WORLD SCIENCE REVIEW
Sighting 17/18 August 1953, MEXICO

Mexican Adventure

By Desmond Leslie

Flying Saucers are still news. World Science Review is frequently asked to publish articles on this contentious topic and we here give, without comment or necessarily agreeing with the opinions expressed, the author's report on one of the investigations made during a recent tour of the Americas, where he addressed numerous lectures and spoke on thirty radio and T.V. circuits about flying saucers.

Small Men

SALVADOR VILLANUEVA is ABOUT forty years old. He has a wife and seven children. He neither smokes nor drinks and is well respected in his Mexico City home. By trade he is a driver owning a car which he plies for hire mainly to tourists.

Breakdown

Between August 17-20, 1953, Villanueva was employed by a couple of Texans to drive them from Mexico City to the Texan Border. After covering about sixty miles successfully, they had just passed Cudata Valley when horrible noises came from the crankshaft and the car ground to a halt. Examination showed that oil had leaked from the differential and it soon became obvious that the car could go no further, not that night at any rate. The Texans were angry. They unloaded their baggage, engaged another car and drove off without saying. Salvado[r] tried to obtain help but there was nowhere for him to do so. He decided there was nowhere for it but to spend the night in his car and arrange repairs in the morning.

Still he felt there was nothing wrong until, as the night descended, his strange new friend betrayed his conversation that he knew far too much for an ordinary man, not only about this world but about others also. He spoke of places and cities and people that made Villanueva a little afraid. Finally, when he around dawn, he asked him the question that was creeping into his mind.

"No," came the answer, "We are not of this planet. We come from one far distant, but we know much about your world."
Of course, he did not believe him right away. At first they seemed to be playing some kind of practical joke on him, and the second man's silent smiles irked him. Several times during the night he accused them of "taking the mickey" out of him.'
Poor Villanueva! By the time dawn broke he was a very confused man.

Invitation

After sunrise, his companions said they must leave. Then they asked if he would care to see their machine. Still faintly hoping to find a conventional aircraft with wings and propellers Villanueva agreed to follow them. They led the way through the bushes across a rather swampy piece of land for about a kilometre. Onwards they walked, his two visitors marching ahead of him. The ground became wet and treacherous. It was sinking into muddy pools, sometimes almost to his knees. But the men in front were not sinking at all. When their grey-clad feet touched muddy pools the mud sprang away from them as if repelled by some invisible force. No dirt ever seemed to come in contact with them or their strange garments and footwear.

He hesitated. The men in front turned and smiled encouragement. So he plodded on his courage and followed through the rain-soaked scrub. Their feet fascinated him. What incredible forces allowed them to walk...what strange forces guarded their perforated belts so glow with their own light each time this happened?
