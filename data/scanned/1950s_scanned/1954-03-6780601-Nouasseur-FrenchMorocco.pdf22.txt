HEAD ROUTING
730th ACG S. Madroom

1. At 2000z, 5 March 1954 the GCI and TP-10D primary search and heightfinder radar at Rioe FL, this unit, were put into operation from Nouasseur Air Base report of unidentified aircraft north of Nouasseur Air Base by Nouasseur Air Base Operations. Base Operations and the GCA unit at Nouasseur reported that several pilots in the traffic pattern had observed lights of unidentified aircraft using no recog-nition of the aircraft at any time but only reports of lights passing overhead. At 2015z a pilot of KC-97 reported seeing on (2) lights appearing to be fighter-type aircraft at 1200 feet altitude and providing evasive action as the first report of aircraft was made by a pilot flying KC-97 (#539 who was certain of a collision course with him at 1200 feet heading 175 degrees at 1918 hours. At 1938 and 1949 hours of 6 March a B-47 (#[illegible]) pilot reported seeing a C-47 (#380) pilot at 2150 hours. At 2152 hours a C-54 reported a bright light, and one of his altitude at 800 feet which was slowing at lights. The pilot of the same C-31 was arriving from Lajes and apparently was not aware that previous sightings had been made. This same pilot has been flying C-47s as a letter pilot.

2. The weather at the time of the reported sightings was as follows: clear, no clouds, no haze, and visibility seven (7) miles.

COMMENTS OF THE PREPARING OFFICER

1. The source of the above information is considered to be reliable inasmuch as the information obtained from personnel flying in the area of the reported sightings and reports of personnel of the Intelligence Officer, SAVA, Nouasseur Air Base, French Morocco.

2. On the sixth and seventh of March the TP-10D Height Finder continued to give an indication of a target of ten thousand (10,000 feet. (III) on CGA height indicator (H) until 0600 hours, 7 March 1954, on F-84 and scrambled and returned to the area where the target was indicated; however, no contact was made by the F-84.

3. It was the consensus of opinion of electronics officers and the Fillo personnel of this unit that the high indication was due to the presence of a high tension electrical line at the exact point on the ground below the target indication.

4. Paragraph three (3) above does not explain the reported sightings by pilots of unidentified aircraft having apparently 1200 feet altitude six (6) to eight (8) miles south of Nouasseur Air Base.
