2. LADD AIR FORCE BASE, ALASKA

2. On 26 February 1951 at [redacted], 2d Sgt. Air Force Group, was interviewed. Sgt. [redacted] stated that he was plac­ed on guard duty at Building 133 at 1430 hours 26 February 1951. At approxim­ately 1715 hours he was at his post at the south side of the building and he observed a large bird, probably a raven, which flew up into the air. As he watched its flight he observed an object in the sky, which at first he thought to be a weather balloon. He reported that this object was at an altitude of about 1,500 feet and passed directly over the Ladd Air Runway. In order to observe it, he had to look at out vertically or at an angle of approximately 50°. This object, which appeared grey, was shaped like a flattened football, having no sharp edges. [redacted] stated that there was not sufficient light to determine if there was metallic glow, judging the altitude at 1,500 feet [redacted] believed its dimension to be 10 to 12 feet thick and about 120 feet across. When first observed it was hovering over the runway and had an oscillating motion similar to that of a wind dropped on a table. It remained in this position for about 45 seconds. Then the object exited a soft "puff" of smoke which formed a ring and accelerated rapidly to the north at a speed far in excess of that of a jet aircraft. This photograph is said to watch in the flight of the object but lost it as it passed toward the general direction of Birch Hill (north). The white smoke ring lasted only a moment. There was no apparent offset on cloud formation. No sound was heard or odor detected. The object was at such distance that [redacted] could not determine any structural details. The witness stated this object appeared similar to another one which he saw in late 1950 and which was observed by at least ten other individuals. [redacted] is an aircraft mechanic with two years military experience in that specialty. He is not a young military person. He is reported to have a 20/20 corrected 1.0 has hobby to radio. He was not fatigued or under the effect of any stimulant at the time of observation. He is considered reliable by officers of his organization. He reported the sighting immediately to the Commander of the Guard.

3. According to the 632d AC&W Squadron, Ladd AFB, there were no reported radar echoes on 26 February 1951 between 1430 and 1730*.
