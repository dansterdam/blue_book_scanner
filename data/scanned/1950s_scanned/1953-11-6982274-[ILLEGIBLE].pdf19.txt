12. Intelligence Officer's Comment: The sighting lasted only two seconds and
could not be verified by other crew members. The weather report for
C1G53 on 19 Nov 53 indicated no storms were known to be in the area of the
sighting. The winds in the upper levels at Westover Station and vicinity on
19 Nov 53 were such that all weather balloons released by this station
would have been carried in the opposite direction of the above sightings.
The weather report at this station state it would be possible for a weather
balloon from Bermuda to reach the position of the sighting and much more
probable than a balloon from this station making such positions. However,
the weather reports indicated and the pilot confirms that there were no
thunderstorms or lightning in the area at the time of the sighting. The
brightness of the object sighted by Capt Coolidge (in his words it resembled
an explosion) and the short duration of the sighting (in clear skies from
9,000 to 9,000 feet) would tend to indicate the object was a light from a
ship at sea or another conventional aircraft. The pilot who reported the
sighting is a senior pilot with more than 3,500 hours and a fighter pilot
who has varied prior to his return to the some of his crew the first
part of December 1953. The pilot stated he had never observed a similar
sighting.

The reporting officer finds no explanation from the reported
sighting and in the absence of further information, believes it would be
very difficult to determine the nature of the object.
