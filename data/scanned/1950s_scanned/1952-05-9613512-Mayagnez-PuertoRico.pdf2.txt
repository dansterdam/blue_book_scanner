8. The objects (2) sighted appeared to be spherical and "orange and shining like the sun" in color. There was no regular formation but one object thought to be larger than the other appeared stationary while one of the two seemed to draw away from the other at a rapid rate of speed then to reappear in close proximity to the larger. There was no exhaust, sound or conventional type of propulsion noted.

b. The objects were sighted between 1900 and 1930Q and the observation lasted between twenty to thirty minutes. Sundown was at 1856Q.

c. The observation was made visually from the ground, elevation approximately 700 to 1000 feet above sea level. Zeiss binocular, 10 x 50 field glasses were used to observe the objects. The observers could not distinguish anything more about the objects when using the glasses except that the partial mention in paragraph a, above, was not discernible to the naked eye and could be seen through the glasses.

d. The observation was made from a high bluff, latitude 18°11'30"; longitude 67°13'30". The point of observation overlooks the city of Mayaguez and Mayaguez Bay. Distance from observation point to sighted object could not be estimated. Heading from point of observation to objects was approximately 270° magnetic.

e. There were five witnesses to the sightings.

Mr. [redacted], a Puerto Rican business man and lawyer, a man of stability and dependability in his community. Mr. [redacted] did most of the observing with the field glasses and it is upon his description that the major part of the report is based.

Mr. [redacted], an American, ex-Air Force pilot, son-in-law of Mr. [redacted]. He is also in business in Mayaguez and impressed the investigator as a person of sound judgement and integrity.

Miss [redacted], a Puerto Rican college student, 20 years old. Miss [redacted] appeared to be a sensible young person, well informed and of mature judgement.

The other two witnesses were Mrs. [redacted], and Mrs. [redacted]. The investigator did not have the opportunity of talking to these people.

It is the opinion of the investigator that all the observers are people of sound judgment and integrity and were not influenced by mass hysteria.

f. The weather conditions at the time of the sightings were described as clear with visibility of approximately twenty (20) miles. Clouds were described as a closed ceiling. Rauncy weather station 25 miles from the scene of the sighting recorded the cloud formation at the time as U-2. Sighting appeared to be situated near the second layer of clouds.

h. Investigations made on the sight following the original sighting revealed no natural circumstance that could adequately account for these sightings.
