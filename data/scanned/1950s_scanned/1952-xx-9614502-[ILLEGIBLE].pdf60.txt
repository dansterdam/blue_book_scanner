1. One bright, smooth appearing, highly polished silver oval with an estimated diameter of 50 feet was observed at estimated altitude of from 500 to 1000 feet and first appeared to have two radio towers (WCN and WBBU) tower mike close observers. At first-sighting the object appeared stationary and appeared to be over the towers. During the sighting the object traveled in the southward and then back upward to approximately its original position. It also moved to some extent in the vertical plane. Its acceleration during these movements was practically instantaneous to a speed which suggested that by human effort than any jet-fighter. At times the object moved slowly and during the time it lowered it appeared to rock on its longitudinal-axis. When the object rockedor rocked into a vertical plane its shape was easily discernable as that of a silver bullet shape. It appeared to be plus or minus 50feet in length. At one point it made a slow spiraling descent during the first 25 feet distance of the observation period, followed by a rapid circle of light haze. The object was not accompanied by any wind or sound. For that 15-20 minutes of the observation the object left an trail of exhaust and made no noise. It's disappearance was described as "like a light being switched off, except a puff of dust". For distance of fifteen miles.

Time of sighting: 1715 to 1825 CDST, 2 Jun 52. Length of sighting: [illegible]
