1. On 3 November 1951, at 0003Z, R-5D #542, on route from Argentina, Newfoundland, to Prestwick, Scotland, reported a sighting of a bright white flare which came up through an undercast. Location of the sighting was at 47° 00' N, 37° 35' W, approximately 60 miles due east of St. Pierre, Gorre Island, a French possession 100 miles south southeast of Ernest Harmon AFB, Newfoundland.

2. Cander Airport advised that no other aircraft, the R-5D was in the area at the time of sighting; Halifax advised that no RCAF aircraft were in the area at the time of sighting; and the US Coast Guard at Argentia Naval Station reported that no aircraft or surface vessels under their control were in the area at the time of sighting.

3. Eistol S-1-10 #2058 was dispatched and reached the area of sighting at 0109Z. A search of the area was made at an altitude of 5,000 feet. (Further let-down was not made due to a second incident described further in this report). The pilots, Captain E. Lilen, reported the area clear, with unlimited visibility, and with an undercast lying to the south. An estimated four surface vessels were sighted in the area. All reported to be small fishing boats. A powerful rotating white beacon was visible at St. Pierre and lights on the Nova Scotia coastline, over one hundred miles away, were plainly seen. The pilot's position was that the C-5D flares may have arisen over the edge of the undercast and had been mistaken by the R-5D pilot for a flare.

4. Both the pilot and co-pilot overheard heavy VHF traffic on "D" channel and, to which HF also verified, transmitting aircraft, were ascertained that some sort of USAi formation of aircraft were near the area. Voices were described as being unmistakably American.

5. To clarify this incident further, a message was forwarded to the Intelligence Officer, at the Pentagon level Air Traffic Intelligence Center. Inasmuch as the only R-5D the carefully interrogated upon landing for all details of the sighting, the following answer was received in response to this request:

"YOUR 0305302. OBSERVER LCG P L JONES PILOT, A MECHANICAL LT L O SPRINGER COPILOT. MOTION FROM 235 KNOTS DURING ESTIMATED 3 SECS SOUTH AT 17 DEGREES NORTH 57 DEGREES 35 MINUTES WEST, AREA OF PYROTECHNIC LIGHT OBSERVED FLARE, ROCKET TYPE WITH TAIL, WHITE LIGHT END FUSE, COLOR WHITE, ESTIMATED MOTION DUMB SHAPED FLARE ARCED 600 TO 700 FT. ELEVATION OF FLARE FROM BELT TO WEST, OBSERVED FLYING ALTITUDE OF 5900 FEET. DURATION OF FLARE RECKONED 3 SECONDS. NOTHING NOTED BOTH ON TOP OF OVERCAST, WEATHER BOARD OVERCAST TOP 5650 FEET. VISIBILITY DARK UNLIMITED. OBSERVED ESTIMATED FLARES 40 DEGREES PORT WING. FLARES SIGNED BOMBING POSITION OVERCAST. NO OTHER AIRCRAFT OR VESSELS KNOWN TO BE IN AREA. SUBSEQUENT CHECK OF OTHER AIRCRAFT OPERATIONS OR SCHEDULED OPERATIONS INVOLVING PYROTECHNICS OR FLARES FIRING IN AREA."

6. While S-1-10 #2058 was investigating the white flare sighting, it was diverted from the area to investigate the following incident:

a. At 01142 FAA Rosling Flight No. 133, on route from Sydney, Nova Scotia, to Bermuda at 10,000 feet reported a downed flare sighted at 0114Z (S Now) at 45° 25' N, 39° 40' W, slightly off the coast from Yarmouth on the northern tip of Newfoundland. Its 20 in diameter was observed lying to the water, flashing its landing lights, but received no response from the water.
