AIR INTELLIGENCE INFORMATION REPORT

1. On 3 April 1953, at 0225Z, in the vicinity of UTM coordinates TQ-3050, Lt. Colonel John D. Dillon, a B-25 pilot flying night armed reconnaissance on a heading of 355 degrees, with an indicated speed of 200 mph, and at an altitude of 5,000 feet, 500 feet on top of a solid overcast, observed a bluish-green object about 200 feet from his aircraft on a level, parallel flight with the aircraft. Heading same as aircraft. The object turned from bluish-green to red and then disappeared. Observed at altitude 4,500 feet for approximately five (5) seconds.

2. 1/Lt Perry observed a white light descending from very high altitude. Aircraft traveling at a very high speed on heading approximately 100 degrees. 1/Lt Perry would not attempt to estimate distance. Object appeared to be white with no noticeable change in color. Approximately five (5) seconds before it disappeared. He estimated that it was observed for approximately five (5) seconds.

3. 2/Lt Vermillion's statements were the same as the above with exception that color was bluish at first, then turned green, then turned red. He observed for approximately ten (10) seconds.

4. A/2C Caruthers stated same as above although he observed object to be green at first, then red then white and went out. Changes of color were simultaneous. Estimated approximately three (3) seconds. Observed from 35 to 40 degree angle above aircraft, falling at very rapid speed. Resembled a meteor. Size approximately five (5) feet in width.

Comments of the preparing officer: Crew was very cooperative. Combat missions completed: 1/Lt Perry, 45 missions; 2/Lt Vermillion, 20 missions; A/2C Caruthers, 12 missions. Information considered reliable.

Comment of the approving officer: Opinion that this was probably sighting of an unusually large meteor. Moon entering third quarter might have prevented object from dissipating to large extent. Color changes due to: 1) composition of meteor; 2) refraction by atmosphere; 3) effect of cockpit and navigation light color on viewer's eyes. No previous report of similar observation. No evidence object was controlled in any manner or anything but unusually large meteor.
