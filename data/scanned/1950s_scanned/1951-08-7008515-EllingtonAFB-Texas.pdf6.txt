AIR INTELLIGENCE INFORMATION REPORT

From: Intelligence, 3605th Nav Sqd., Ellington AFB, Houston, Tex.
Date: 1-Aug-51

Statement of [redacted] [redacted] 3605th NAF Squadron, Ellington Air Force Base, Houston, Texas

On the morning of the 26th of August 1951 I was sitting in a car 2 miles west of Ellington Air Force Base, Houston, Texas, it was 0530 hours. Something had gone wrong with the car and [redacted] had gone to get someone to pick us up to the base. I was just sitting there, half asleep, when suddenly I noticed something like a star. It was too bright to be a star and not bright enough to be Venus, which was, I believe, the only other bright object that night. This object had a reddish tint to it would. It was located near three stars in a vertical line, being off to the side of the middle one when I first noticed it at 0530 hours. In the course of half an hour I traced its complete roughy in a triangle. It remained quite near the middle star to above the third-star and from there in a straight line to several degrees left of the lowest star. At this time 0600 hours [redacted] returned and I pointed it out to him. It had returned to near the point where I first observed it. As we watched it began the same movement again. I could only approximate the distance - if it were a light of an average size plane perhaps several thousand feet - if a larger light then higher up. Also, is considered motionless for a minute at a time when and suddenly start moving again. It was still there, if there were an exhaust trail it was invisible - there was no sound. It was still there when we returned to the base.

[redacted]
Capt, 3605th NAF Squadron
