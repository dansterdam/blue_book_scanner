CASE #11

15 July 1952                                Norfolk, Virginia

A B-4 type a/c, piloted by 2 airline pilots, was approaching Norfolk, Virginia, about 20-25 miles out on the B3 leg of the range, at 8,000' when 6 unidentified objects were first sighted approaching e/c on a heading of about 60 deg at approximate 2,000'.

When the objects reached a point under & slightly aft to the right of the a/c, one of the observers saw them roll up on edge and, not make a turn in an arc, instantly shoot off ca a heading of 270 deg, rolling back to a flat position. This change of direction was made with the suddenness of a bullet ms ricocheting off a stone wall after the abrupt change of direction two more of the objects appeared from behind and to the right of the B-4 and joined formation. o-- 20l-.

The speed of the discs was estimated at well over 1,000 mph. When first seen, they were glowing on the top side with an intense amber-red light, and resembling a glowing red hot coal. Their diameter was approximately 100' (estimated) and they appeared to be perfectly circular. They approached the B-4 in a narrow echelon formation, almost in trail, and appeared to decelerate just before changing direction. With the deceleration a dimming of their intense glow was noted, and immediately after changing direction and flattening out, the glow disappeared entirely. They reappeared at once, glowing brilliantly again, and began climbing on a heading of 270 deg. Their glow was extinguished again, this time in sequence rather than simultaneously, and they were lost from view.

ATIC Comments: Five Jet a/c were in the vicinity of Langley AFD at the time of observation. After various checks with surrounding AF agencies, it was concluded that objects were not the 5 jet a/c. Other checks for known airborne objects were made with negative results. Sighting occurred at 2012 EST on 14 July 1952 for a period of 15 seconds.
