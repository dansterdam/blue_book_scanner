Dear Dr. Hynek:

Attached herewith is a series of photographs submitted by Mr. [redacted] Director of CRIFO and "The UFO," a UFO publications. I am certain this gentleman needs no introduction, inasmuch as we have discussed him before. It may also interest you to know that his case file, containing material dating back a number of years, was submitted to Headquarters, Air Force, as a representative example of a "private" UFO research organization.

I submit these directly to you for evaluation rather than to our photo people because of the photo-astronomical aspects which, I feel, we do not have the degree of knowledge that your staff possesses.

Attached also is a copy of a letter from Mr. [redacted] relating, briefly, the circumstances under which these photos were taken. We shall keep this case in suspense, which we will call the "Bleton Case," pending return of your analysis and comments.

At first glance, I am prompted to remark that in my younger days as a "keen teen," I attempted to attach a "Brownie" camera to one end of a cardboard tube telescope and photograph the moon. I recall an over-anxious object appearing on one of the photos, after I accidently knocked the telescope completely around where it faced a light bulb. The camera being offset, tripped, with the result that it photographed the opening of the telescope through the long, black-painted tunnel of the tube.

It would have been interesting if the moon was in the same photo with the "object." As it is now, it could be anything photographed in the darkness. The absence of stars or other images in the background is significant, in view of the fact that if the sky was photographed with any degree of magnification, other celestial objects or lights would undoubtedly appear on the plate.
